## 1. Gustation

⑴ Gustation: The perception of five tastes, together with olfaction

⑵ Types of taste

> ① Sweet: Sugar molecules

> ② Bitter: Quinine

> ③ Salty: Sodium ions

> ④ Sour: Hydrogen ions

> ⑤ Umami: Glutamate and inosinic acid. Discovered by Professor Ikeda in 1908. A savory taste, or the taste of udon broth

> ⑥ Kokumi: Researchers in Japan are making sustained efforts to identify this taste

⑶ Characteristics of taste

> ① Around the age of five, humans distinguish between what can and cannot be eaten

<br>

<br>

## 2. Structure of the Tongue

⑴ Epiglottis: Recognizes sour tastes and induces a vomiting response to food

⑵ Tongue: Several types of papillae are present on the surface of the tongue (see below)

⑶ Lingual papillae

> ① Fungiform papillae: Shaped like mushrooms

> ② Foliate papillae: Shaped like leaves

> ③ Vallate papillae: Shaped like ramparts

> ④ Hundreds of taste buds are embedded in a single papilla

⑷ Taste buds

> ① A single taste bud contains 50–150 taste cells

> ② Structures: Basal cells, taste cells, taste pore, microvilli

⑸ Taste cells

> ① Taste cells are epithelial cells, not neurons

> ② Taste cells form synapse-like structures with nerves to transmit signals

> ③ Tight junctions prevent ion leakage

> ④ Graded potentials

## 4. Signal Transduction

⑴ Sweet, bitter, and umami tastes

> ① These pathways take time because they involve G proteins

> ② The mechanisms for sweet, bitter, and umami tastes share a second-messenger signaling pathway but differ in their G protein-coupled receptors

> ③ 1<sup>st</sup>. Sweet, bitter, and umami molecules reach the tongue

> ④ 2<sup>nd</sup>. A G protein activates PLC (phospholipase C)

> ⑤ 3<sup>rd</sup>. PLC converts PIP2 to IP3

> ⑥ 4<sup>th</sup>. IP3 releases Ca2+ stored in the smooth endoplasmic reticulum

> ⑦ 5<sup>th</sup>. Ca2+ promotes neurotransmitter release

> ⑧ 6<sup>th</sup>. The neurotransmitter transmits a neural signal to a gustatory afferent neuron

> ⑨ Sweet receptor: Composed of T1R2 and T1R3

> ⑩ Bitter receptors: Composed of T2Rs

> ⑪ Umami receptor: Composed of T1R1 and T1R3

⑵ Salty taste: There is no separate taste receptor

> ① 1<sup>st</sup>. Na+ reaches the tongue

> ② 2<sup>nd</sup>. Na+ passes through an amiloride-sensitive sodium channel

> ③ 3<sup>rd</sup>. Na+ entering the cell activates voltage-gated Ca2+ channels through membrane depolarization

> ④ 4<sup>th</sup>. Ca2+ promotes neurotransmitter release

> ⑤ 5<sup>th</sup>. The neurotransmitter transmits a neural signal to a gustatory afferent neuron

⑶ Sour taste: There is no separate taste receptor

> ① 1<sup>st</sup>. H+ reaches the tongue

> ② 2<sup>nd</sup>. H+ passes through an amiloride-sensitive sodium channel and inactivates K+ channels

> ③ 3<sup>rd</sup>. K+ entering the cell activates voltage-gated Ca2+ channels through membrane depolarization

> ④ 4<sup>th</sup>. Ca2+ promotes neurotransmitter release

> ⑤ 5<sup>th</sup>. The neurotransmitter transmits a neural signal to a gustatory afferent neuron

⑵ Even for the same receptor, the resulting behavioral response differs depending on the type of taste cell in which the receptor is expressed ([ref](https://www.columbia.edu/cu/zukerlab/Publications_files/2005NatureMueller.pdf)).

> ① To investigate how mammals perceive taste, Ken L. Mueller, Nicholas J. P. Ryba, Charles S. Zuker, and colleagues conducted an experiment using phenyl-β-D-glucopyranoside (hereafter PBDG).

> ② PBDG is a substance that tastes bitter to humans and binds to and activates hT2R16, a human bitter taste receptor (a type of [GPCR](https://jb243.github.io/pages/1424)).

> ③ In contrast, the ordinary mice used in the experiment showed avoidance responses to other bitter substances but no clear preference or avoidance response to PBDG.

> ④ **Transgenic group 1.** Sweet-cell expression group: Mice expressing hT2R16 in taste cells that normally express sweet receptors

> ⑤ **Transgenic group 2.** Bitter-cell expression group: Mice expressing hT2R16 in taste cells that normally express bitter receptors

> ⑥ The control group's relative water consumption (= PBDG / (PBDG + pure water)) generally remained around 50% even as the PBDG concentration increased.

> ⑦ In the sweet-cell expression group, relative consumption rose above 50% as the PBDG concentration increased.

> ⑧ In the bitter-cell expression group, relative consumption fell below 50% as the PBDG concentration increased.

## 8. Theories of Taste Perception

⑴ Labeled line hypothesis (<span style="color: red; font-weight: bold;">rejected</span>)

> ① Each stimulus has a one-to-one correspondence

> ② Advantage: Highly precise

> ③ Disadvantage: Requires a receptor for every stimulus → Not feasible in practice

⑵ Roughly labeled line + population coding (<span style="color: green; font-weight: bold;">accepted</span>)

> ① Responds approximately to similar stimuli

> ② Highly imprecise, but readily accommodates many stimuli

> ③ Final discrimination is determined by integrating the information in the cerebrum
