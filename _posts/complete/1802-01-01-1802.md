## **1. Convergence of sequences and basic properties**

⑴ What it means for a real sequence (a<sub>n</sub>)<sub>n∈ℕ</sub> to converge to a real number a

> ① For N ∈ ℕ, for all n ＞ N

> ② For every positive ε, **there exists** an N such that eventually ㅣ a<sub>n</sub> - a ㅣ ＜ ε

⑵ Given a sequence (a<sub>n</sub>) and an increasing sequence of natural numbers n<sub>1</sub> ＜ n<sub>2</sub> ＜ ···, the new sequence (a<sub>n<sub>k</sub></sub>)<sub>k∈ℕ</sub> is called a **subsequence** of (a<sub>n</sub>).

⑶ **Exercise:** Prove the following.

> ① If eventually a<sub>n</sub> = a, then (a<sub>n</sub>) converges to a.

> ② If (a<sub>n</sub>) converges to a, then the set {a<sub>n</sub>} is bounded.

> ③ Suppose (a<sub>n</sub>) → a and (b<sub>n</sub>) → b. If eventually a<sub>n</sub> ≤ b<sub>n</sub>, show that a ≤ b.

> ④ If (a<sub>n</sub>) → a and eventually a<sub>n</sub> ≤ b, then a ≤ b.

> ⑤ If (a<sub>n</sub>) → a and also (a<sub>n</sub>) → b, then a = b. That is, the limit of a convergent sequence is unique.
