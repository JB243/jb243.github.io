## 1. Importance

⑴ Cases Where Contacting Professors Is Important

> ① Contacting professors is common in engineering fields.

> ② You may be asked during an interview whether you have contacted professors.

> ③ Contacting professors is particularly important at Ivy League universities, where professors tend to have greater authority in admissions decisions.

> ④ Contacting professors is particularly important in engineering programs where students begin research immediately without laboratory rotations.

> ⑤ Even if you are rejected by the admissions committee, a professor you contacted may offer you an opportunity to enter a PhD program after enrolling in a master's program.

> ⑥ Contacting professors offers practical benefits, such as understanding their personalities, checking funding availability, learning about open positions, assessing the laboratory atmosphere, and making a positive first impression.

> ⑦ Even after receiving admission offers, contacting professors can help you decide which university to attend.

> ⑧ Since you may incorporate discussions from your initial contact into your Statement of Purpose (SOP), your chances of admission may increase if the professor you contacted serves on the admissions committee.

> ⑨ You can ask professors for advice on your SOP.

> ⑩ You may receive an offer to transfer from a master's program to a PhD program during a meeting with a professor : This is because the PI's funding availability is one of the most important factors determining whether such a transfer is possible.

> ⑪ For certain PhD programs, such as Harvard's Organismic and Evolutionary Biology (OEB) PhD program, contacting a potential PI is required before applying.

> ⑫ Contacting professors can help you assess your competitiveness as an applicant.

>> ○ If you receive responses from nearly every professor you contact, you can consider yourself a highly competitive applicant.

>> ○ Professors generally read their emails but often do not respond.

⑵ Cases Where Contacting Professors Is Less Important

> ① Contacting professors tends to be less important at top-ranked universities and universities on the West Coast, where institutional admissions systems have greater authority.

> ② In fields such as biology, chemistry, and mathematics, contacting professors is generally less important because students participate in laboratory rotations and are selected by admissions committees.

>> ○ Even within the biological sciences, contacting professors may be important in bioengineering programs, as it is in engineering schools.

## 2. Procedure

⑴ Step 1. Selecting Professors

> ① Criterion 1. Some students work better with male professors, while others work better with female professors.

> ② Criterion 2. Chinese professors are generally not recommended.

> ③ Criterion 3. Full professors are preferable to newly appointed professors.

> > ○ Tenure Track : Includes assistant professors and associate professors. In some cases, the advisor may need to be the first author of publications, and there is a risk of not obtaining tenure. Tenure-track professors tend to conduct research actively to secure funding.

> > ○ Tenure : Tenured professors often aim to conduct research with significant societal impact or substantial academic depth.

> ④ Criterion 4. Choose a professor who has sufficient time remaining before retirement.

>> ○ Professors in the United States generally work until around age 65 and then retire.

>> ○ However, professors who remain active in research do not necessarily retire at a fixed age.

> ⑤ Criterion 5. The professor's research area should align with your research interests.

>> ○ Review the professor's publications from the past 3–5 years (usually available in their CV) and investigate their recent funding.

>> ○ When reviewing a professor's recent publications, focus on papers where the professor is the corresponding author (typically listed as the last author).

>> ○ Following a professor's Twitter account can help you stay informed about recent academic trends.

>> ○ Incoming graduate students are highly likely to participate in their professor's recently funded research projects.

⑵ Step 2. Contact Email Format

> ① Structure

>> ○ Pay attention to grammar, capitalization, and formatting.

>> ○ The shorter and more concise the email, the better.

>> ○ Aim for 5–7 sentences.

>> ○ Use formal greetings such as "Dr. LAST_NAME_of_PROFESSOR" or "Prof. LAST_NAME_of_PROFESSOR." Avoid informal greetings such as "Hey."

>> ○ Mention one of the professor's recent publications.

>> ○ Use appropriate technical terminology.

>> ○ Describe your research interests.

>> ○ Include a polite closing such as "Thank you" or "Sincerely."

>> ○ Attach your CV and unofficial transcripts.

>> ○ Be aware that certain attachments may cause your email to be classified as spam.

> ② Examples

<br>

```
Dear Professor [THE LAST NAME OF THE PROFESSOR],

My name is [MY NAME]. I will graduate from [MY UNIVERSITY] in South Korea with a [MY DEGREE] in [MY MAJOR]. At the same time, I work as [MY CAREER]. I am planning to apply for [MY APPLICATION PLAN].

I have been given courseworks for [THE COURSES I HAVE TAKEN]. Also, I performed [MY FIRST RESEARCH EXPERIENCE] and [THE EXPERIENCE STATUS]. Furthermore, I have performed [MY SECOND RESEARCH EXPERIENCE]. Currently, I'm interested in [MY CURRENT RESEARCH EXPERIENCE] because [THE REASON WHY I'M INTEREST IN THIS]. My goal is [MY MISSION].

Recently, I was intrigued by your research article, [ONE OF THE PROFESSOR'S RECENT ARTICLES]. I was fascinated because [THE REASON WHY I'M INTERESTED IN THIS]. I would like to participate in research in this field because it will help me complete my mission.

Would it be possible to meet with you to further discuss [THE PROFESSOR'S INTERESTS] and my possible involvement in the research? I attached my CV and unofficial transcripts. I look forward to hearing from you.


Sincerely,

[MY NAME]
[MY STUDENT ID]
[MY PHONE NUMBER]
[MY E-MAIL ADDRESS]
[MY WEB SITE]
```

⑶ Step 3. Sending Emails

> ① If contacting professors is necessary, reach out to 2–3 faculty members before submitting your application : However, contact only one professor at a time.

> ② Check each professor's laboratory website carefully for specific contact requirements, such as required attachments or statements indicating that the professor is not accepting inquiries.

> ③ Send emails only on weekdays in the United States.

> ④ Since professors often check their emails shortly after arriving at work, it is advisable to send your email between 8:00 and 9:00 AM local time.

> ⑤ You may also contact current graduate students with questions.

>> ○ Graduate students sometimes participate in admissions committees.

>> ○ Korean graduate students reportedly receive many inquiries from Korean applicants.

> ⑥ You can contact professors even after the application deadline.

⑷ Step 4. Contact Interviews

> ① It is beneficial to discuss specific research topics.

> ② Be prepared for either casual conversations or serious academic discussions.

> ③ Asking plenty of questions is recommended.

> ④ Interviews are often scheduled about one month after the initial email, so it is advisable to prepare meeting materials during that time.

> ⑤ Professors who encourage prospective students to contact them in advance on their laboratory websites are more likely to respond to initial emails and arrange interviews.

⑸ Step 5. Contacting Professors After Admission

> ① In fields such as biology and mathematics, where laboratory rotations are common, students reportedly send brief emails of approximately 1–2 lines when contacting professors about rotations.

## 3. Types

⑴ Contacting Professors Before Admission

> ① When contacting professors is optional (e.g., in programs with laboratory rotations) : The purpose is to increase your chances of admission and inquire about available positions.

> ② When contacting professors is mandatory : The professor you contact may become your prospective academic advisor.

> ③ Some departments encourage contacting professors even when it is not mandatory (e.g., evolutionary biology), while others consider it unnecessary or explicitly prohibit it.

⑵ Contacting Professors After Admission

> ① Admitted students contact professors to select their academic advisors.

> ② This is particularly common in departments that require laboratory rotations.
