 ⑻ **Operation 6:** Vector Product (Cross Product)

> ① Definition of cross product: Operation satisfying three conditions for v, w ∈ ℝn:

>> ○ v × w is a continuous function of the pair (v, w)

>> ○ v × w is perpendicular to both v and w: (v × w) **·** v = (v × w) **·** w = 0

>> ○ If v and w are linearly independent, then v × w ≠ 0

> ② Direction of cross product: follows right-hand rule.

>>>> ○ Invariance under scaling transformations: The validity of the BAC–CAB rule under scaling transformations follows directly from the fact that both dot and cross products are linear with respect to the magnitude of each vector.

>>>> ○ Invariance under coordinate rotations: Since $\mathbf{B}\times\mathbf{C}$ and $\mathbf{A}\times(\mathbf{B}\times\mathbf{C})$ transform under coordinate rotations in the same manner as $\mathbf{A}$, $\mathbf{B}$, and $\mathbf{C}$, the validity of the BAC–CAB rule is preserved, thereby completing the proof.

## **5\. Two Approaches of Newtonian Mechanics**

⑴ Newtonian Analysis

> ① Mechanical analysis using vector quantities such as force and acceleration

⑵ Lagrangian Analysis

> ① Mechanical analysis using scalar quantities such as energy

> ② L (Lagrangian) = T (kinetic energy) - U (potential energy)

⑶ Hamiltonian Analysis

> ① Hamiltonian H = Σ p<sub>i</sub> q<sub>i</sub>′ - L

>> ○ p<sub>i</sub>: Generalized momenta

>> ○ q<sub>i</sub>: Generalized coordinate

> ② In a single-particle, single-system case, the Hamiltonian can be expressed as H = T (kinetic energy) + U (potential energy)

> ③ Hamiltonian in quantum mechanics

⑷ Hermitian Analysis

> ① Hermitian in quantum mechanics

<br>

<img width="651" height="94" alt="스크린샷 2025-09-06 오후 2 11 12" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/850b1548-51bb-4b6b-a636-1ee6d91763fe" />

<br>

> ② The Hamiltonian is always a Hermitian operator.

> ③ The eigenvalues of a Hermitian operator are always real.

>> ○ If $Av=\lambda v$, then

$$\langle v,Av\rangle=\langle v,\lambda v\rangle=\lambda\langle v,v\rangle$$

>> ○ On the other hand, since $A$ is Hermitian,

$$\begin{aligned} \langle v,Av\rangle &=\langle A^\dagger v,v\rangle\\\ &=\langle Av,v\rangle\\\ &=\langle\lambda v,v\rangle\\\ &=\lambda^\*\langle v,v\rangle \end{aligned} $$

>> ○ Therefore,

$$\lambda=\lambda^\*$$

>> ○ Hence, all eigenvalues of a Hermitian operator are real.

⑸ Laplace–Beltrami Analysis

> ① Laplace–Beltrami Operator

>> ○ Measures how much the value of a function at a point on a curved surface differs from its surrounding values.

>> ○ In flat Euclidean space, the Laplace–Beltrami operator reduces to the standard Laplacian:

$$\Delta f=\frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x^2}+\frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial y^2}$$
